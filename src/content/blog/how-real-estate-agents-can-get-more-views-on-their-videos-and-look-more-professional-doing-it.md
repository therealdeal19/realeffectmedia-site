## How to Get More Views on Your Real Estate Videos

### Start With Keyword Research, Not the Camera

Before you film anything, find out what people are actually searching for. Open YouTube, type in something like "moving to [your city]" or "[your neighborhood] homes for sale," and see what autocomplete suggests. Those suggestions are real searches from real people. Build your video ideas around them instead of guessing.

### Write Titles That Combine a Keyword With a Reason to Click

A title like "Market Update" gets ignored. A title like "Austin Real Estate Market Update for 2026: What Buyers Need to Know Before Fall" gets found and gets clicked. Lead with the keyword, then add the specific value someone gets from watching.

### Treat Your Description Like Part of the SEO Puzzle

Write a real description, not just a one line caption. Two to three hundred words with your primary keyword near the top, a short summary of what the video covers, and a few relevant links back to your site or listings. This is one of the easiest, most overlooked ways to help YouTube understand what your video is about and who to show it to.

### Build Playlists Around Topics, Not Just Upload Order

Organize your channel into a few clear playlists, like neighborhood guides, buyer tips, and market updates. When someone finishes one video, a related video in the same playlist should be the obvious next click. This keeps people watching longer, and YouTube rewards channels that keep viewers on the platform with more visibility over time.

### Stay Consistent

One or two videos a week, posted on a predictable schedule, will outperform a burst of five videos followed by three months of silence. Batch filming once or twice a month makes this realistic even with a full schedule of showings and closings.

### Repurpose Everything

Every long form video can become two or three short clips for Reels, TikTok, and YouTube Shorts. This puts your face in front of more people without adding extra filming time, and it drives traffic back to your main channel.

## How to Look More Professional on Camera

### Fix Your Audio Before You Worry About Your Camera

Viewers will forgive average video quality far more than they'll forgive bad audio. A simple wireless lavalier microphone makes a bigger difference than almost anything else you can buy, and it's one of the cheapest upgrades available.

### Shoot Steady, Shoot Landscape

Use a tripod or a small gimbal so your footage doesn't shake. Always film in landscape for YouTube. These two habits alone make a video look noticeably more professional, even filmed on a phone.

### Dress and Present Like You're Meeting a Client

You wouldn't show up to a listing appointment in a wrinkled t-shirt, so don't film like that either. Dress the way you would for an in person meeting with a serious buyer. It signals credibility before you've said a single word.

### Open With a Hook, Not a Slow Intro

Don't start with "Hey guys, welcome back to my channel." Start with the thing that matters most to the viewer, right away. Something like "Home prices in this neighborhood just dropped for the first time in two years, here's what that means for you." Then introduce yourself.

### Clean Up Your Captions

YouTube auto generates captions, but they routinely mangle real estate terms, neighborhood names, and local landmarks. Taking five minutes to fix them not only looks more polished, it also helps YouTube's algorithm correctly understand and rank your content.

### End With a Clear Next Step

Every video should end with a specific call to action, whether that's subscribing, checking out a related video, or reaching out for a consultation. Don't leave viewers wondering what to do next.
