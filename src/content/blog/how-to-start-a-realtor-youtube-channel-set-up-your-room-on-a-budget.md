### Step 1: Pick Your Room

You don't need a dedicated studio. A home office, a spare bedroom, or a corner of your living room works. Look for three things:

**Quiet.** Stay away from the kitchen, the washer and dryer, and rooms that face a busy street.

**Soft stuff.** Rugs, curtains, couches, and bookshelves soak up echo. An empty room with hard floors sounds like you're recording in a bathroom. If your room echoes, throw down a rug and hang some curtains. That fixes most of it for next to nothing.

**Room behind you.** Sit three to four feet away from the wall. That depth makes your video look way more professional and gets rid of harsh shadows on the wall behind you.

If you can leave everything set up, even better. The easier it is to start filming, the more videos you'll actually make.

### Step 2: Cheap Lighting That Looks Expensive

Lighting is the biggest difference between a video that looks homemade and one that looks like a real estate brand. And it's the easiest thing to fix cheaply.

**Free option: use a window.** Sit facing a window so daylight lands on your face. Never put the window behind you or the camera turns you into a shadow. Cloudy days give the softest, most flattering light.

**Budget option: two softbox lights.** A basic two light softbox kit from Amazon usually runs under $60 to $80. Put one light at about 45 degrees to one side of the camera, a little above eye level. Put the second on the other side, turned down lower, to fill in shadows. This setup is used on thousands of YouTube channels and looks great.

**Also good: a small LED panel.** Compact LED panels are cheap, don't get hot, and let you adjust brightness and color. Use one as your main light next to a window.

**Skip the ring light if you can.** They work, but they put a ring shaped reflection in your eyes and make the light look flat. A softbox looks more natural for the price.

**Match your lights.** Set every light to the same color temperature (daylight, around 5600K, is the easy choice) and turn off the overhead lights. Mixing warm bulbs with daylight makes your skin look weird on camera.

### Step 3: Audio (Don't Skip This One)

People will watch a video that looks a little rough. They won't watch one they can't hear.

Your phone's built in mic picks up echo and room noise. A **wireless lavalier mic** that clips to your shirt fixes that. Budget options start around $20 to $40, and popular picks like the DJI Mic or Rode Wireless GO are worth it if you plan to film a lot. Clip it about a hand's width below your chin.

Before every session, record ten seconds and play it back with headphones on. Listen for echo, the AC humming, or your shirt rubbing against the mic.

### Step 4: Use the Camera You Already Have

Your phone is good enough to start a realtor YouTube channel. Seriously. Modern iPhones and Android phones shoot sharp 4K video.

- **Use the back camera,** not the selfie camera. It's a lot sharper.
- **Get a tripod with a phone mount.** They're cheap, and they're what make your shots look steady and professional.
- **Put the camera at eye level** or just slightly above. Filming from below your chin never looks good.
- **Film horizontally** for long videos and vertically for YouTube Shorts.
- **Wipe the lens.** A smudged lens is the number one reason agent videos look blurry.

### Step 5: Build a Background That Says "Trusted Local Agent"

Your background should look clean and put together, with a little personality. Some easy ideas:

- A bookshelf with a few books, a plant, and one or two decor pieces
- A lamp in the corner behind you for warmth and depth
- A framed photo of your city or a local landmark
- Your brand colors in a pillow, print, or accent piece

What to hide: laundry, clutter, TVs, and anything with a logo that isn't yours.

### Step 6: Get Your First Videos Out

Now for the part that actually grows your channel: consistency.

- **Batch film.** Record two to four videos in one sitting so you're not setting up every week.
- **Use bullet points instead of a script.** You'll sound more natural. If you'd rather read, a free teleprompter app works great.
- **Wear solid colors.** Thin stripes and busy patterns flicker on camera.
- **Start with questions your clients already ask you.** What's it like to live here? Which neighborhoods are best for families? What does it cost to move here? Those make perfect first videos.
- **Keep a checklist next to your setup:** mic charged, lens wiped, lights on, phone on Do Not Disturb.
